## Two lifecycle problems to fix before broadening buy permission

### 1. A breakout can lose its confirmation at the next publication

**Why it matters.** A trader who followed Sunday’s trigger expects Monday’s qualifying close to have a consequence on Tuesday. The live reader selects the latest dated plan and starts its price path after that plan’s reference date. It uses the previous decision for comparison. The simulator follows the original selection through later bars. Both behaviors make sense separately, but they can answer different questions about the same trade. [Live path selection](../app/modules/market_universe/market_universe_service.py#L893) · [Confirmation](../app/modules/stock_details/decision/entry_applicability.py#L324) · [Simulation](../app/modules/backtesting/backtesting_execution.py#L233)

**Reproduced.** I supplied identical breakout terms, a Monday close above the trigger with sufficient volume, and an in-range Tuesday quote to the actual path builder and entry evaluator. With Sunday 15 November as origin, the evaluator found the 16 November confirmation and returned `APPLICABLE`. With the 16 November publication as origin, the same quote returned `AWAITING_CONFIRMATION`. Price coverage was complete in both controlled paths. A new publication may legitimately change the terms or invalidate a thesis. The current projection gives the reader little help distinguishing replacement from continuation.

What size of opportunity might this affect? Of the 76 archived Potential Buys, **63 were breakout plans**. In a price-and-volume check against their original terms, 55 had a usable next-session bar and eight closed above the trigger on enough volume with their low above the stop. Five of those eight saw the following session’s range touch the original entry zone, and only one opened inside it. Daily bars cannot establish a tradable quote or the order of intraday events. These eight cases are a starting set for reviewing actual plan replacements.

**Small fix first.** Use the existing previous decision and publication ID to say “Yesterday’s plan was replaced” when the evidence establishes that fact. Reserve “confirmed” for the original trigger, original volume baseline and actual confirming session. Compare consecutive real publications to count setups that were confirmed, replaced, invalidated or expired before a later entry could be assessed.

**Larger change only if the count warrants it.** Carry a short-lived reference to the originating immutable plan for still-compatible setups. Keep its original trigger, stop, expiry and confirmation date. Require the latest decision to permit the entry and recheck market restrictions, corporate actions, price path and present quote. A withdrawn plan, a breached stop or materially changed terms ends that lineage. Exercise Sunday issue → Monday confirmation → Tuesday in-range, gap above range, intervening stop, later SELL, closure and correction across all three sessions. Use identical cases in live projection and replay before claiming executable continuity.

### 2. Pulse history can miss the close or record the wrong owner

**Why it matters.** On 17 November, Pulse displayed a calculated score and said comparable history would appear as snapshots accumulated. The daily job attempts its history capture after market finalization but **before** it schedules the normal cache rebuild. If the decision winner is still the previous session, capture returns `not-finalized`. The rebuild paths refresh the universe and observe entries, but have no corresponding Pulse-history completion call. This is a possible explanation for missing history on a given day. The public page alone cannot identify its cause. [Daily job](../app/jobs/ingestion/ingest_daily_market_prices.py#L369) · [Rebuild](../app/jobs/market_cache_rebuild.py#L340) · [Observed Pulse page](https://stockwealthbd.com/market-pulse)

**Reproduced.** I exercised the capture service with the archived v3 rows and in-memory repository doubles. A selected previous-day winner returned `not-finalized`. A same-day updating v3 winner produced a proposed history write labelled **v4**. A withdrawn same-day winner also reached that write when the selected ID still matched. The capture derives strategy, threshold and input schema from running defaults. Its guard checks date and selected ID. The history reader uses running defaults too. No database writes were involved in this check. [Capture and identity](../app/modules/market_pulse/market_pulse_snapshot_service.py#L62) · [Winner comparison](../app/modules/market_universe/market_universe_service.py#L461) · [History reader](../app/modules/market_pulse/market_pulse_service.py#L415)

**Fix.** Complete capture after the required durable decision winner is available, including on a retry or a Redis-miss recovery. Bind the operation to the expected generation and its own strategy, threshold and schema, plus the Pulse algorithm actually used. Reject updating or withdrawn winners. Treat duplicate capture of identical inputs as success. If a same-date correction has a different revision, record the conflict explicitly and withhold the comparison until the chosen vintage is resolved. The existing unique insert and population fingerprint provide a starting point. Verify a delayed build, two retries, a same-date correction, a rolling v4/v5 deploy and missing historical inputs. Keep gaps in the five-session series visible. [Snapshot persistence](../app/modules/market_pulse/market_pulse_snapshot_repository.py#L57) · [History comparison](../app/modules/market_pulse/market_pulse_service.py#L450)

## Which trading rules should change?

### Market evidence and a weak index

The attachment correctly identifies a market-wide restriction. V4 checks confidence, completeness and decision date. The producer uses 50 index closes for the current average and a 51st for the previous phase comparison. Completeness also checks valid prices, finality, aligned dates when supplied, and valid advancing/declining counts. In production the date list itself comes from finalized DSEX summaries. A session missing from both lists can evade that cross-check. A fresh-looking latest number is insufficient evidence that the whole baseline is sound. [Regime calculation](../app/modules/stock_details/decision/market_regime.py#L439) · [Session source](../app/modules/market_data/market_data_repository.py#L331) · [Current veto](../app/modules/stock_details/decision/engine.py#L223)

With complete inputs, v4 calls the market bearish when DSEX is more than 0.5% below its 50-session average and fewer than 55% of advancing-or-declining stocks rose. Even a day when 52% of movers rose can therefore carry a bearish label. That is the specific judgment worth testing. [Current label rule](../app/modules/stock_details/decision/market_regime.py#L301)

| Market evidence at decision time | Proposed behavior to evaluate |
| --- | --- |
| Current finalized DSEX close, valid matched history, valid breadth | Use the measured regime. Classify broad weakness from both trend and participation. Keep unchanged stocks outside the advancing/declining ratio. |
| Same sound index history, breadth alone absent | Record “breadth unavailable” and use a cautious index-only state. Keep qualifying stock setups visible. Evaluate conditional entry for the explicitly guarded subset in shadow. The existing classifier can return bullish when breadth is absent, so bypassing the final veto alone gives the wrong semantics. |
| Missing, stale, nonpositive or unfinished close, broken baseline, conflicting revisions or calendar evidence | Withhold fresh-entry authority. Keep the last dated thesis readable. A conflicting or malformed value warrants a distinct diagnostic from an unreported count. |
| Index below its band, participation mixed | Test a caution state. A high-quality, liquid pullback can remain a watch with an explicit level. Test entry separately against completed price evidence, risk and geometry. A genuinely weak market can still have an empty list. |

**Measured sensitivity.** On identical archived dates, retaining the 0.5% band and requiring advancing breadth of **40% or less** for the bearish label changed **9 of 34** bearish sessions to neutral. The other **25** remained bearish. This measures reclassification. All 50 dates had a numeric breadth ratio, so the test cannot estimate the frequency of a breadth outage. The existing shadow market policy is a different experiment. It requires two consecutive sessions for restriction and has its own conditional-entry rules. Use a reason for missing market evidence that can distinguish breadth alone from a broken price history. Bind that fact to the captured input and its hash. [Current classifier](../app/modules/stock_details/decision/market_regime.py#L286) · [Two-session candidate](../app/modules/stock_details/decision/market_regime.py#L247)

### How to judge the result

- **Reader comprehension.** Take a breakout awaiting a close, a pullback awaiting a better price, a last-close observation, a current in-zone plan, a market-only watch and an invalidated plan. In English and Bangla, a reader should be able to say whether the stock is a buy idea, what to do now and what would change the answer. AVOID belongs to the prospective buyer. SELL belongs to the holder when the published decision calls for it.
- **Decision quality.** Keep separate counts for listed stocks, eligible stocks, valid raw geometry, permitted dated theses, qualified watches, current applicable entries, modeled executions and verified fills. Record zero-idea dates. Keep primary reasons exclusive and report overlapping blockers separately. Include median turnover, missed sessions, breadth denominator and source, entry timing, and outcome coverage. On 17 November the dashboard showed 261 rising, 76 falling and 44 unchanged, with 45 of 426 unclassified. Pulse reviewed 209 in its eligible cohort. Each denominator answers a different question.
- **Safety and usefulness together.** Check exact boundaries at 40%/55% advancing breadth, ±0.5% index distance, 2.3%/3.0% volatility, 1.2× first-target R/R, 8% stop width and final permitted session. Check a gap past the range, no trading, a corporate-action discontinuity, two independent vetoes and a late response after withdrawal. For ranking, compare watch-list relevance and actionable entries separately. For outcomes, use effective-dated execution rules, costs and actual coverage. A next-close markout answers a different question from a fill.
